2026 Pikes Peak: A Hard Week, Three Finishes

The whole week fought us — mechanical and electronics gremlins for all three drivers, and a last-minute car change for one — but every O'Maley Racing entry made it to the summit. Here's how it went, and where we go from here.

Let's be straight about it: 2026 was a week that fought us from the first practice day. Every O'Maley Racing driver spent it chasing gremlins instead of chasing setup — and on Pikes Peak, where you get one run on race day, lost practice time is time you never get back.

Rodney, #65

Mechanical issues every single practice day limited Rodney to one run a day. That's nowhere near enough to corner a setup and find real time, so improving on the 2025 run was always going to be an uphill battle. He still got the #65 to the summit on race day, finishing 5th in Open Wheel on the full course in 10:28.629 — 21st overall.

Earl, #19

Earl fought an electronics gremlin all week. He got more than one run most days, but none of them were clean — and without a good run to learn from, it was tough to really understand the car or find the setup to get everything out of his Kumho V730 tires. He still made the summit, taking 6th in Open Wheel at 11:41.540 (51st overall) — but there was more in that car than the week let him show.

David Meyer, #24

Just days before the race, a catastrophic motor failure forced David out of the O'Maley Special open-wheeler and into his Palatov — a car about 500 pounds heavier, and a completely different animal to learn on short notice. The switch also moved him into a different class, which meant running in different practice groups and less support from the O'Maley Racing camp than a normal year. He got it done and finished anyway, taking 8th in Pikes Peak Open at 11:01.887 (40th overall).

Three finishes, and a summit

Here's the thing: after a week like that, all three O'Maley Racing entries finished and made it to the top of America's Mountain. Given everything that went sideways in the days before, just getting all three cars to the summit felt like a win.

And none of it happens without the people who show up and help us at Pikes Peak every single year. That crew is the reason we make the start line at all — thank you.

Pikes Peak is one and done. One run, no do-overs. So we'll take 2026 on the chin, put our heads down, learn everything we can from it, and come back swinging for a great 2027.
